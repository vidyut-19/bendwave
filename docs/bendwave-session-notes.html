why the delay-on-pause bug mattered

In Web Audio, stopping an AudioBufferSourceNode stops new input, but leaves whatever audio is already orbiting inside a DelayNode → feedback → DelayNode ring buffer. Flushing and remounting fresh silent nodes on pause eliminates ghost echoes — and stops Track 1's reverb or delay tail from popping back out the instant you press Play on Track 2.

((2)) the "harvested dna" sampler callbacks · live motifs · fred again.. playbook

Most DJ samplers are treated as an afterthought — a static folder of generic risers, claps, and airhorns that have nothing to do with the records you are actually playing. The idea you floated at the end of today's session flips that completely on its head: a living sampler populated directly from the tracks in your set.

Imagine you are three songs deep. Track 2 has an impossibly wet, punchy kick you want to keep in your back pocket. Track 4 has a textured 808 with a particular saturation you love. Track 6 drops a one-bar UKG bass wobble that makes the room lift off. Track 9 has a half-bar vocal phrase that catches your ear. Instead of letting those tiny moments vanish once the fader crosses, you harvest them onto your 8 center pads and carry them with you for the next twenty minutes.

In live electronic music — think Fred again.., Four Tet, Overmono, or Floating Points — bringing back a vocal chop or bass wobble from fifteen minutes earlier over a completely different record is called a callback. It transforms a sequence of separate tracks into a single coherent story with recurring characters. Almost no DJ software is built to let a selector do this casually on the fly.

fig 2.1 — pillar 6: stem-to-pad harvester flow ~85% of dsp plumbing already in vibedeck v1.4

The remarkable engineering twist is that nearly all the heavy DSP is already running in vibedeck today. Demucs already separates local tracks into sample-aligned DRM / BAS / OTH / VOX stems. Our beatgrid already knows every downbeat to the millisecond. And signalsmith-stretch — the MIT WASM time-stretcher we already bundled for key lock — supports independent playback rate and semitone pitch shift. That means a 1-bar bass wobble harvested at 132 BPM in 5A can lock cleanly onto a 126 BPM track in 8A twenty minutes later without clashing a single note.

((3)) instrument vs. daw: why not an open-source ableton protecting live flow from scope creep

Once you picture pulling kicks, wobbles, and vocal slices straight out of live tracks, a natural question pops up: should we bring in an open-source Ableton-style engine? It is a tempting thought, and it is also the single most important architectural line to draw clearly before building another line of code.

The honest answer is no — keep vibedeck an instrument, not a DAW. Digital audio workstations (Ableton Arrangement View, Ardour, Logic, LMMS) are designed for offline composition: multi-track timelines, piano rolls, VST plugin chains, complex bus routing, and microscopic mouse editing. The moment you drop DAW complexity into a live DJ console, it stops being playful. You stop listening to the room and start administering software.

the 95% rule of live ableton sets

Even artists who famously perform on Ableton Live ignore 95% of Ableton on stage. They are not opening synth patch browsers or drawing MIDI piano rolls mid-set — they are living entirely inside tempo-locked audio clips, a filter, a delay, and physical velocity pads. A focused Stem-to-Pad Harvester captures 100% of that live magic with virtually zero UI bloat.

VIBEDECK — FIELD ESSAY & ARCHITECTURE NOTES CRAFT · TASTE, MUSCLE & NEXT STEPS
((4)) the curator's craft: taste vs. mixing muscle why crate-digging is the hard 80%

You mentioned feeling that your real strength as a DJ lives on the curation and crate-digging side — appreciating small sonic details, textures, and pockets — while still building the physical muscle memory of live mixing and sampling. That is not a weakness to apologize for; in DJ culture, it is the single hardest thing to develop.

Beatmatching, phrase counting, bass swaps, and filter sweeps are mechanical skills. Put in a handful of focused sessions and your hands simply learn them. Hearing why a particular wet kick, an 808 decay, or a two-bar vocal pocket belongs right after another record cannot be taught by a tutorial or faked by software. Nearly every respected selector — Ben UFO, Floating Points, Jamie xx, Four Tet — started as an obsessive listener whose taste pulled their technical hands forward, not the other way around.

The best part about how vibedeck is built right now is that it lets your ear lead while quietly training your hands by osmosis. Every time you run an automated transition and watch the Boeing 737 LO and FLT dials rotate across an 8-bar phrase, you are watching exact DJ mechanics happen in real time on your own tracks — and the instant you reach out and grab a knob, the machine steps aside.

((4.2)) four low-pressure reps for mixing muscle 15 min · darty shortlist · zero homework
01

Watch the Autopilot Move the Dials

Drop your Darty Shortlist folder in, open ((SET PLAN)), load step 1 & step 2, and press ▶ RUN. Don't touch anything the first time — just watch when the bass swaps at bar 5, how far the FLT wedge turns, and how the waveforms line up.

02

Steal Just One Knob Mid-Transition

Next transition, hit ▶ RUN again — then grab just the outgoing LO knob or the crossfader yourself. Touch-to-override immediately surrenders that one control to you while the engine handles the rest. One hand, zero panic.

03

Ride the Arrangement Phrase Countdown

Skip ▶ RUN and watch the deck header countdown (e.g. DROP → BREAK in 4.0). Practice pressing PLAY on Deck B right as the bar hits 1. Ninety percent of clean club transitions are simply starting the incoming record on the right downbeat.

04

Keep the K.O. II Simple at First

Start with only four muscle-memory moves on the hardware: Play, Cue, 4-Beat Loop Roll, and Echo Out. Once those four feel automatic without looking down, adding harvested kicks and vocal callbacks on the remaining pads will feel like second nature.
